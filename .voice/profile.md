## Editorial patterns — learned from live drafts (added 2026-07-18)
From the extension-layer post (`2026-07-18-safe-extension-stack`), a live redraft after Doug pulled a cheap-dunk framing (a "one vendor = half the vuln list" hook aimed at Amasty). Three Layer-2 rules for a surface that names vendors constantly:

1. **Concentration is structural, not moral.** When data clusters on a named party, don't manufacture a villain for the hook. Diagnose the cause (here: vendor size + researcher scrutiny + honest disclosure are what land a CVE in a public list) — which is fairer *and* the stronger take. A cheap dunk is the cynical snap the soul forbids: SOUTH collapsing past honest-diagnosis into contempt.
2. **Hold both sides on named vendors; never sanitize to a non-position.** The corrective to #1 is not toothlessness. State the real value (cheap, capable, does what rivals can't) *and* the real exposure (concentrated, well-probed surface) as one decision seen two ways. Pure praise and pure caution both fail — pole tension made concrete.
3. **The terminal LIFT often means questioning the premise, not just the tactics.** Strongest house landing: zoom out from "which X is safest" to "do you need X at all." Tactics → premise is the fact→interpretation vector applied to the close ("Scan what you keep. But keep less.").
